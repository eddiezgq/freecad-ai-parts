## How students take a lecture

1. Hour 1: read this lecture's courseware on the learning platform (web interactive courseware; animations and virtual labs work in the page).
2. Hours 2–4: in the course click “Digital factory lab: lab NN (open the digital factory)” to enter this factory. In the left sidebar “Teaching → Labs & tasks”, open this lecture's lab.
3. Read the lab guide first, then do tasks 1–5. Do it yourself first, then submit; the hub's suggestions show only after submitting (compare mode).
4. After class write the lab report with a screenshot of your score on the lab page, and hand it in to this lecture's assignment in the course.

## How teachers run a lecture

1. Teach hour 1 with the courseware; press S for the presenter view with speaker notes.
2. Before the lab, follow section III “Preparation” of the lab guide and set up the scenario in the “Teacher” block at the top of the lab page (students cannot see it).
3. During the lab, approve the actions students start that cost money in “Plant Insight Hub → Action inbox”.
4. In the last 10 minutes, discuss against the hub's suggestions: who chose the same as the hub, who did not, and why.

## When the textbook and the platform numbers differ

The textbook's worked examples follow “the joint reducer order added by the robot plant”; on the platform the same main order shows Harbor Cranes as the customer, and stop times and queues may also differ.
Use the textbook's method and formulas and recompute with the platform's numbers.

## Two-week course project

Practice week at the end of the term. The teacher uses the “Scenario director” to play two or three scenes a day; each group decides and starts actions in the command center in its assigned roles, and on the last day reviews and defends: how much this order finally made, and where the money leaked. See “Teachers only: scenario director and teacher console”.

## Schools without internet access

You can run the local version of this factory in a computer lab or intranet: one computer runs the whole factory, teaching mode and the reducer demo plant by default; students connect with a browser and sign in with their names. The lab administrator installs and starts it following the local version's instructions.

Note: the local version signs in by name, and **everyone who signs in has teacher permissions** — they can see the lab page's “Teacher” settings and the class table, compare mode does not apply to them, and they can approve actions they started.
In class, ask students to use only their own names and not touch the “Teacher” settings; when you need compare mode, use the online digital factory (sign in with a WenQuest account).
